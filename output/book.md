# Pay Me on UPI: How Digital Payments Changed Small Business in India

*A friendly guide for first-time small-business owners in India*

## Chapter 1: The Change at the Counter

Welcome, my friend, to the wonderful journey of running your own shop. I see you standing behind your new counter, full of hope but perhaps a little worried about how to handle the daily rush of customers. Let me tell you about Ramesh, who runs a modest corner grocery store just down the lane. A few years ago, Ramesh spent his evenings surrounded by torn paper notes, searching for small coins to give back as change, and manually writing down every single transaction in a dusty paper register. Customers would often walk away simply because Ramesh did not have change for a five-hundred-rupee note. Cash, which once seemed like the only way to do business, had become a daily headache. Ramesh was losing business and feeling exhausted at the end of every single day. He felt like he was running in circles, spending more time counting coins than talking to his customers.

Then, a quiet revolution arrived at Ramesh's counter. He decided to try UPI, or the Unified Payments Interface. This is a free national payment system that moves money instantly from a customer's bank account directly into your bank account using a mobile phone. This wonderful system was launched on 11 April 2016 by the National Payments Corporation of India, also known as NPCI, under the regulatory oversight of the Reserve Bank of India, which we call the RBI [1]. At first, Ramesh was hesitant because he had always relied on paper money, but he soon realised that his customers were changing. They no longer wanted to carry heavy wallets; they wanted to pay with their mobile phones. He realised that keeping up with his customers was the best way to keep his business alive.

To welcome these customers, Ramesh placed a QR Code, or Quick Response Code, on his counter. This is a black-and-white square barcode printed on a counter stand that customers scan with their phone camera to pay your shop in seconds. Think of it as a digital address for your shop's cash register. When Ramesh set up his code, he joined a massive national family. As of July 2026, there are 741 banks live on the UPI network [2]. The scale of this system is truly breathtaking and shows how much trust the nation has placed in it. In July 2026, UPI processed a record monthly volume of 2,366 crore transactions and a record monthly value of ₹29.88 lakh crore [2].

You might wonder if this system is meant for small shops like yours in quiet neighbourhoods. The answer is a resounding yes. To help set up these digital payment tools in villages and small towns, a special scheme was started by the central bank. Approximately 56.86 crore QR codes have been deployed under this scheme to help merchants in every corner of our country [3]. This means that whether you run a tea stall, a roadside cart, or a neighbourhood grocery store, you are part of a modern payment network. You do not need to be a big merchant in a glittering mall to use this technology. It is built for you, the heartbeat of our local markets.

For Ramesh, the benefits were immediate. He no longer had to worry about receiving counterfeit notes or spending his precious time counting coins. The money went straight into his bank account, safe and secure. The digital payment stand on his counter became as essential to his business as the lightbulbs hanging from his ceiling. It was no longer a chore to manage, but a friendly tool that kept his business running smoothly, ensuring he never lost a customer who forgot their wallet. Imagine the relief of knowing that every single transaction is recorded perfectly without you having to lift a pen. You too can experience this peace of mind. By adopting these tools, you are not just changing how you receive money; you are upgrading your shop to match the speed of modern India. You are opening your doors to a wider world of convenience and safety.

Takeaway: Embracing digital payments simplifies your daily business operations and ensures you never lose a customer due to a lack of physical change.

## Chapter 2: Setting Up Your Counter

Now that you understand how digital payments can transform your business, let us talk about how to set up your counter safely and easily. When Ramesh first started, he made the mistake of using his personal bank account for his shop transactions. This made his bank statements very confusing because his household expenses were mixed with his shop earnings. To solve this, Ramesh set up a Merchant Account. This is a specialised payment profile meant for commercial sales that tracks business income separately from your household family savings. Setting this up is very simple; you only need your bank account, basic identity documents, and a trusted payment app on your mobile phone. This simple step gives your business its own identity and helps you understand exactly how much profit your shop is making each month.

Many new shop owners worry about the cost of accepting digital payments. Let me reassure you. Small merchants including street vendors receiving up to ₹1 lakh per month through UPI QR codes under the Person-to-Person-Merchant (P2PM) category will continue to enjoy zero transaction charges, also known as the merchant discount rate—which is simply the fee that payment companies usually charge shop owners for processing digital payments—on all transactions [1]. If your business grows larger, a nominal MDR of 0.4% will apply only to P2M transactions above ₹2,000, and for transactions of ₹75,000 and above, the MDR will be capped at ₹300 per transaction [1]. This fee is shared among the banks and app providers who keep the payment network running safely. It is a very small price to pay for the immense convenience and safety that the system offers to your growing business.

Once Ramesh realised the value of a dedicated business profile, he placed a Soundbox on his counter. This is a small, battery-powered speaker on your counter that speaks out the exact rupee amount received in your local language the moment a customer pays. Before he got this speaker, Ramesh had to stop serving, wash his hands, and check his phone for a text message every time a customer claimed they had paid. Now, during the busy evening rush, the speaker loudly announces the payment, allowing Ramesh to serve customers without any delay or worry about fake payment screens. At the end of the day, all the money collected through his store's QR code is bundled and deposited into his main bank account. This automatic daily transfer is called Settlement, which ensures your hard-earned money is safely moved from the digital screen into your actual bank account. This automated deposit means you do not have to carry bags of cash to the bank every evening.

Safety is another common concern, but a few simple rules will keep your money completely secure. The most important rule to remember is about your UPI PIN. This is a secret four- or six-digit password known only to you that is entered strictly when sending money out of your bank account. You must never enter this password to receive money from a customer. If someone asks you to enter this password to accept a payment, it is a scam. To protect you, NPCI provides an AI/ML-based fraud-monitoring solution—which is a smart computer system that automatically learns to spot dishonest activity—to all banks for generating alerts and declining suspicious transactions [2]. This means there is a digital guard dog watching over your transactions at all times.

If you ever face any digital fraud or cyber incident, the government has created strong systems to help you. To facilitate citizens in reporting cyber incidents including financial frauds, the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) has launched the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal and National Cybercrime Helpline Number 1930 [2]. By keeping these safety rules in mind, Ramesh has run his store for years without a single security issue. You can set up your counter with the same confidence, knowing that the system is designed to protect your hard-earned money. You are never alone in this digital journey, as help is always just a phone call away.

Takeaway: Setting up a dedicated business profile and using audio confirmation tools will keep your transactions smooth, separate, and secure.

## Chapter 3: Turning Payments into Growth

As your shop grows, you will realise that accepting digital payments does much more than just make your daily transactions easier. It actually builds a solid foundation for your business's future. For many years, small shops in India operated entirely in the dark. According to official data from the central bank, about 97 per cent of micro, small, and medium enterprises operate in the informal sector [1]. Because they dealt only in cash, they had no way to prove their income to banks. This made getting a business loan almost impossible. In fact, by the financial year 2021, only 19 per cent of credit demand from these small businesses was met formally, leaving an estimated ₹80 lakh crore in credit demand completely unmet [2]. This meant that many hardworking shop owners had to rely on informal lenders who charged extremely high interest rates, making it very difficult to grow.

Ramesh was once part of this informal group, unable to get a loan to expand his grocery store. Everything changed when he started accepting digital payments, which created a Digital Trail. This is the permanent electronic record of all money coming into and going out of your shop, which acts as official proof of your business health. These digital records make it easy for banks to see how well your business is doing, removing any doubts they might have had about lending to you [3]. This means banks can now easily see and trust how much money your shop is actually making, without requiring complicated paperwork. It is like having a clean, honest diary of your success that speaks directly to the bank manager on your behalf.

With this digital proof of his sales, Ramesh was able to apply for formal credit. He is not alone in this journey. Between 2020 and 2024, the share of micro and small enterprises accessing credit through scheduled banks rose from 14% to 20%, while medium enterprises saw an increase from 4% to 9% [2]. Even street vendors have benefited immensely from these programmes. For instance, as of July 30, 2025, over 96 lakh loans amounting to ₹13,797 crore have been disbursed to more than 68 lakh street vendors under the PM SVANidhi scheme [4]. Among these, nearly 47 lakh digitally active beneficiaries have conducted over 557 crore digital transactions worth ₹6.09 lakh crore, earning a total cashback of ₹241 crore in the process [4]. These numbers show that the financial system is opening its arms to small businesses like never before.

Ramesh used his first business loan to buy a larger refrigerator and stock more inventory. He paid his wholesale suppliers instantly using digital payments, which earned him early-payment discounts and built deep trust with his vendors. He also began using his payment app's built-in business dashboard to track his daily sales. By looking at these simple charts, Ramesh could easily spot his peak shopping hours and identify which items were selling the fastest. This allowed him to manage his stock perfectly and avoid wasting money on products that sat on the shelves. He felt like a true modern businessman, making smart decisions based on real facts rather than just guessing.

By adopting digital payments, Ramesh saw his business flourish. He is part of a massive wave of successful merchants across India. About 57 per cent of small merchants reported an increase in sales following digital adoption [5]. When you place that digital payment stand on your counter, you are not just accepting money; you are building a bright, verifiable future for your business. You are stepping out of the informal shadows and into a world of formal growth, where banks are eager to support your dreams. With every digital payment you accept, you are laying a brick for a stronger, more prosperous tomorrow.

Takeaway: Building a verifiable electronic record of your daily sales opens the doors to formal bank loans and long-term business growth.
